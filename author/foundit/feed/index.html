Whether you own a standby generator for your home or a commercial unit supporting critical operations, this checklist will help you understand what it takes to keep your system ready year-round.

Why Generator Maintenance Matters

Generators are mechanical systems with moving parts, fuel systems, electrical components, and control panels all working together. Over time, wear and tear is inevitable. Without regular service, small issues quietly grow into expensive problems.

Routine maintenance helps:

• Ensure reliable startup during outages
• Extend the lifespan of your generator
• Maintain fuel efficiency
• Prevent unexpected breakdowns
• Protect connected appliances and equipment

A generator that sits unused for months without inspection can develop battery failure, fuel degradation, oil breakdown, or corrosion. The storm does not wait for you to be ready.

Monthly Maintenance Basics

Even if you have professional service scheduled annually, there are a few simple checks that should be performed regularly:

1. Visual Inspection
Look for leaks, corrosion, loose wires, or debris around the unit. Clear away leaves, dirt, and anything that may restrict airflow.

2. Check Fluid Levels
Oil and coolant levels should be inspected regularly. Low fluids can cause overheating and engine damage.

3. Inspect the Battery
Battery failure is one of the most common reasons generators fail to start. Check for corrosion on terminals and ensure connections are tight.

4. Exercise the Generator
Running the generator periodically keeps components lubricated and ensures the system functions properly. Many standby generators are programmed to self-test weekly, but manual verification is still important.

Quarterly and Annual Professional Service

While visual checks are helpful, professional maintenance goes much deeper. At Performance Energy Services, our technicians perform comprehensive inspections that include:

• Oil and filter changes
• Fuel system inspection
• Load testing to verify power output
• Electrical system checks
• Exhaust system inspection
• Software and control panel diagnostics

Load testing is especially important. A generator might start, but that doesn’t guarantee it can handle your full electrical demand. Proper testing ensures your system performs under real-world conditions.

Seasonal Considerations in Louisiana

Louisiana weather brings its own challenges. Heat, humidity, and storm seasons can stress generator systems.

Before Hurricane Season:
Schedule a full inspection. Replace worn components and confirm fuel systems are secure.

During Hot Summers:
Ensure cooling systems are functioning properly to prevent overheating.

After Major Storms:
Have your generator inspected even if it ran successfully. Extended operation can accelerate wear and tear.

Preventive care is far less costly than emergency response.

When to Consider Replacement

Even with excellent maintenance, generators do not last forever. If your unit is more than 10 to 15 years old, requires frequent repairs, or struggles to meet your energy needs, replacement may be the smarter investment.

Performance Energy Solutions can evaluate your current system and recommend a properly sized upgrade designed for efficiency and long-term reliability.

A generator is not just a piece of equipment. It protects your comfort, your business operations, your inventory, and in some cases, your safety. Regular maintenance ensures that protection is never compromised.

A generator is a powerful safety net during power outages—but like any mechanical system, it can experience issues over time. Ignoring warning signs can lead to costly repairs, unexpected downtime, and frustration when you need power the most. At Performance Energy Services, we help homeowners and businesses identify problems early and keep generators running reliably.

Sign 1: Unusual Noises

Generators are not silent machines, but certain noises can indicate a problem. Grinding, knocking, or rattling sounds may signal loose components, worn bearings, or engine issues. If you hear anything abnormal, it’s time for a professional inspection.

Sign 2: Difficulty Starting

If your generator struggles to start, fails to start, or requires multiple attempts, this could indicate battery issues, fuel problems, or a failing starter. Regular testing and maintenance prevent these issues from leaving you powerless during outages.

Sign 3: Irregular Power Output

Flickering lights, inconsistent voltage, or frequent tripping of circuit breakers can mean your generator isn’t delivering stable power. These issues can damage appliances and electronics if left unchecked. Our technicians perform load testing to ensure optimal performance.

Sign 4: Visible Leaks

Leaks of oil, fuel, or coolant are not normal and can lead to serious damage or safety hazards. Even a small leak should be addressed immediately to prevent engine failure or fire risk.

Sign 5: Excessive Smoke or Odors

Smoke or unusual odors from your generator could indicate exhaust problems, fuel system issues, or internal engine trouble. Prompt inspection can prevent more serious damage and reduce the risk of hazardous fumes.

Why the Right Generator Matters

A generator that’s too small won’t meet your energy needs, leaving you with limited functionality during outages. Conversely, an oversized generator can be costly, inefficient, and take up unnecessary space. Selecting the right unit ensures:

  • Optimal Performance: Your generator powers everything you need without overworking.

  • Energy Efficiency: Proper sizing reduces fuel consumption and operational costs.

  • Longevity: Generators operating within their intended range last longer and require fewer repairs.

Step 1: Assess Your Power Needs

Start by identifying what you need to power. For a home, this might include lights, refrigerators, HVAC systems, and medical devices. For a business, consider computers, servers, machinery, or essential production equipment. Performance Energy Solutions can help you calculate the total load and recommend a generator that meets your needs precisely.

Step 2: Choose the Right Type

Generators come in various types, each with its advantages:

  • Portable Generators: Compact, flexible, and easy to move, ideal for temporary or emergency use.

  • Standby Generators: Permanently installed and automatically activated during an outage, perfect for homes and businesses seeking uninterrupted power.

  • Industrial Generators: Heavy-duty units designed for large commercial or industrial operations requiring continuous and reliable energy.

Step 3: Consider Fuel Options

Generators can run on natural gas, propane, or gasoline. Each fuel type has unique benefits:

  • Natural Gas: Cleaner-burning and convenient if you have an existing gas line.

  • Propane: Clean fuel with easy storage, often used for standby generators.

  • Gasoline: Widely available and suitable for portable units but may require more frequent refueling.
